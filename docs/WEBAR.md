# WebAR parallax window

The GitHub repository contains three implementations of a photograph reconstructed with Apple's SHARP: native iOS (`ios/`, ARKit and MetalSplatter), Mattercraft (`mattercraft/`, Zappar and Spark), and WebAR (`src/ar/`, 8th Wall, Three.js, and Spark). WebAR is the development target. Mattercraft's multiple-window placement is separate from the single-window WebAR experience; the sweep-band and periphery effects are shared through its shader graph files.

## Run and review

Run `npm install` if needed, then `npm run dev`. Vite serves HTTPS on port 5174 for phone camera and motion access. The engine copy step runs automatically and preserves the bundled 8th Wall engine and license.

- `/ar.html`: phone experience. Start AR, allow camera/motion, move slowly forward and back until scale calibration completes, then tap anywhere on the camera view to place the memory. Placement uses the phone's horizontal heading and the tracker's estimated floor; it never waits for a surface hit. The matching spot is 1 m ahead of where you tapped, and the photograph waits beyond it, sized to fit the sideways screen at 80% of its height from there. Turn the phone sideways: a deck of photographs of people who lived there rises along the bottom of the screen. Walk up until the photograph fills the guide frame fixed on the screen; holding it there sends Peter SooHoo's card flying into the frame, and its landing starts the activation (see **Match cut** and **People**). There is no activation tap. Reset clears the placement and restores the original for the next placement.
- `/memory.html`: camera-free preview of the **same** activation controller and splat modifier, seen through a phone held sideways (64° across) at the matching height. Tap the photograph to play. Tuning controls are hidden by default; add `?controls=1` for the timeline, scene controls, and particle-flow sliders. There, **Walk forward** walks the phone from the placement to the photographer's spot, where the match starts the activation as on the phone. `?walk=1` starts 1 m in, at the spot; `?t=` implies it. Replay restores the dormant photograph.
- `/index.html`: existing camera/framing tuning preview.

AR and memory preview accept `?splats=full|50|25` (default `50`) and `?fx=none|band|periphery|band,periphery`. AR also accepts `?dpr=1` and `?debug=1` to show tracking/render diagnostics. Diagnostics are hidden by default; camera, placement, activation, and error prompts remain available. Memory preview accepts `?t=1.15` to freeze a stage on load and `?fxTime=3` to freeze the ambient sweep phase for comparisons.

Both pages accept `?spillStrength=6.3&spillAngle=31&spillSoftness=60` (see **Ground spill** below) and `?depthStretch=1&parallaxGain=1`; either gain above 1 breaks the match cut. Depth stretch ranges from 0.5 to 4; parallax strength ranges from 1 to 4. The memory preview also provides live sliders for both. Both default to 1×, which keeps the street's proportions. Depth stretch scales distances behind the portal plane along its normal, keeping the street's ground on the floor. Parallax strength is a camera-dependent shear of the street behind the portal; it makes distant content follow the phone, so it is not physical. Larger gains consume SHARP's limited viewing baseline faster, so its reconstruction quality still limits extreme viewpoints.

`?flatEffectStrength=0.25` scales dreamlike effect changes during the flat stage (range 0–1, default 100%). The **Flat dream effects** slider provides the same live control: 0% disables the flat-stage modulation and 100% reproduces the previous full-strength behavior. Size growth/shrinkage, brightness boost, and opacity reduction are interpolated from their neutral values, so reducing strength does not shrink or darken the base image. The gain smoothly reaches 100% as depth opens; the final 3D effect tuning is unaffected.

The **Particle flow** panel at `/memory.html?controls=1` provides eight live sliders. **Preview ripple** repeats just the color transition; **Pause ripple** holds its current frame. Scrubbing the activation timeline or pressing Replay ends the loop. Editing a slider while outside the color stage starts the ripple preview automatically. Reset restores only the flow settings, and Copy link saves a reproducible tuning URL with the current inspected frame and scene controls. On small screens the panel moves into the scrollable bottom card; collapse it to access the scene controls.

| Parameter / URL key | Default | Range | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `particleCount` | 11,516 | 0–60,000 | Number of spray particles; 0 hides the spray. |
| `particleSizeMin` | 3.4 px | 0.2–24 px | Smallest particle at the crest. |
| `particleSizeMax` | 5.1 px | 0.2–24 px | Largest particle at the crest. |
| `rippleDiameter` | 1.01 (101%) | 0.5–2.5 | Maximum circular ripple diameter relative to the photo diagonal. |
| `transitionWidth` | 0.14 (14%) | 0.04–0.6 | Width of the particle crest and color reveal; scales with the photo diagonal. |
| `flowStrength` | 0.35× | 0–3× | Amount of particle drift, lift, and surface refraction. |
| `flowSpeed` | 1.55× | 0–3× | Speed of swirling and turbulence; the 2.7-second color sweep keeps its timing. |
| `rippleTurbulence` | 1.75 (175%) | 0–2 | Irregularity of the circular front; 0 gives a smooth circle. |

The size boundaries carry each other along when crossed so minimum never exceeds maximum. All eight URL parameters also work on `/ar.html`. The spray reuses a fixed particle buffer and changes its draw count; every density prefix covers the whole image. Diameter, width, strength, speed, and irregularity update the shared photograph/splat/particle wave together. Even a small diameter or broad transition finishes revealing the full photograph smoothly before expansion.

## Live phone tuning and preparation

The phone AR view has a collapsed **Tune** button. The menu docks below a resized camera canvas, rather than covering the memory; closing it restores the full camera area. Canvas resizing updates the XR camera projection and render buffer together. Tuning gestures are handled by the panel and never trigger placement or activation.

In portrait the sliders use two columns with a scrollable area. In landscape, the dock rearranges into four columns and keeps a usable slider area. Resize writes run on the next animation frame to avoid resize-observer errors when opening, closing, or rotating the view.

- **Match:** walk distance (placement to the matching spot), match height (the spot above the floor), image distance (spot to photograph), match frame (the share of the sideways screen's height the matched photograph spans), dolly zoom, match tolerance, and match hold. A line under the Match and Scene sliders shows what scene size and these imply: the matching height, the photograph's size and how much it grows as it opens, the portal's size and distance, how far behind the spot the photographer stands, and where the street begins.
- **Scene:** scene size, ground spill, spill angle, spill edge blur, depth stretch, parallax strength, and flat-stage dream effects.
- **Particles:** the eight particle/ripple parameters listed above.
- **Timing:** color, flat hold, and depth durations.

**Original** restores the dormant photograph at the existing placement; **Replay** plays the full activation; **Loop ripple** repeats the color stage. The activation scrubber holds any frame while the ambient effects continue animating. **Defaults** restores the shared preset, and **Copy link** includes all 24 tuning values without a frozen playback/effects clock. All parameters work as URL keys on AR and memory preview. The address bar follows the tuning at most every quarter second, because Safari throws an error past 100 address updates in 10 seconds, which dragging a slider used to reach. The same phone dock can be reviewed without a camera at `/memory.html?arControls=1`.

Layout and scene tuning update transforms and shader uniforms; they do not rebuild the splat generator, shader, or particle buffers. Photo geometry scales its existing UV-mapped plane, and the shared physical dimensions update the photograph, splats, particle wave, aperture, and tap target together.

First-use work happens before activation: photographs and SOG data load first, then the actual GL renderer warms the color, depth, and final states offscreen. Preparation awaits Spark's generation/sorting, `renderer.compileAsync`, texture uploads, and a WebGL2 GPU fence. The AR camera feed and tracking can run while this happens, with a **Preparing memory effects…** prompt; scene rendering/placement/activation wait until preparation succeeds. In preview it happens before the activation button or scrubber becomes available. A preparation error leaves activation disabled and visible errors intact. Uniform-only tuning does not need another shader warmup. The dormant splat mapping stays resident at zero reveal after placement so replay can reuse the prepared resources.

Placement primes the dormant mapping again before enabling activation. It drains any queued automatic update or in-flight sort first, waits for the placed mapping's sorting to finish, and uploads its ordering texture before the GPU fence. This prevents Spark's early-returning `update()` from making activation ready while a prior sort still runs. `npm test` covers readiness/failure handling, overlapping sorts, placement-time anchoring, the floor-standing street, tuning endpoints, and uniform-only shader reuse.

## Match cut

The activation starts when the viewer stands at the matching spot, phone sideways, with the photograph lined up in the guide frame (`src/photographer.js`). Every size and distance follows from scene size `s`, the dolly, the share of the screen the matched photograph covers, and the capture: SHARP's assumed 30 mm lens gives a 60.0° × 46.8° frame, with half-angle tangents of 0.5769 and 0.4327. The numbers below are for the defaults and a phone showing 64° across a 19.5:9 screen.

- **The spot:** `walkDistance` (1 m) ahead of the phone at placement and `matchHeight` (1.55 m) above the estimated floor, where most people hold a phone up to frame a view. The photographer's eye was 1.39 m × `s` (1.04) = 1.445 m above the street, so the street sits 0.104 m lower than the spot inside the stage and its ground stays on the floor. The flat photograph still matches pixel for pixel, because the flat splats keep their pixels; seen from a little above where it was shot, the street settles that much lower as it opens, the near ground most. At `matchHeight` = 1.39 × `s` the cut is the photographer's own.
- **Photograph:** seen from the spot, fits inside the sideways screen at `matchFrame` (80%) of its height, `photoDistance` (0.8 m) beyond it: 0.53 of the photographer's frame, 49% of the screen's width, 0.49 × 0.37 m. A 4:3 photograph on a 19.5:9 screen reaches the top and bottom first, so the whole photograph and the guide frame stay in view. (An earlier version covered 75% of the screen's area, which on a phone meant 75% of its width with the top and bottom cut off.) Placement measures the phone's own field of view, so the share holds on any phone; until then the layout assumes the screen above. The photograph never comes nearer than the portal's 95%.
- **Dolly:** `dolly` (0.3, settled on a phone after trying 0.2 and 0.4) stands the photographer's capture camera 0.3 × 0.56 × 3.09 m = 0.52 m behind the spot. Dolly 1 restores the tap version, whose viewer stood 1.80 m in front of the photographer, 0.56 of the way to where the street began; 0 matches the photographer's view exactly. The offset is measured against the street's start, as the pivot is, so the same dolly looks the same in any reconstruction, and it leaves the street starting at least 0.5 m ahead of the spot.
- **Portal:** grows from the photograph's frame to keep content at three times the street's starting distance (9.64 m from the photographer) the same size in it: 1.059 of the photographer's frame, so the window opens ×1.99, to the full width of the screen and past its top and bottom. It stands on the floor 1.55 / (1.059 × 0.4327) = 3.38 m past the spot, 4.13 m wide and 3.10 m tall.
- **Street:** begins 2.97 × `s` = 3.09 m from the photographer, 2.57 m from the spot, so its first 0.59 m spills out in front of the portal.

Distant things always appear at the photographer's angles, wherever the viewer stands nearby, so a photograph smaller than the photographer's frame must grow as it opens: at 0.53 of the frame, by ×1.88 with no dolly. The dolly is a dolly zoom relative to the window. Content at the pivot keeps its size in the frame, nearer content grows in it, and farther content shrinks: at 0.3 Peter SooHoo, standing a quarter beyond the street's start, grows 9% in the frame, the buildings shrink 3–4% and the sky 5.6%. These numbers hold for any reconstruction, since the offset and the pivot both scale with the street's start. A sideways phone at the spot sees the street only from about its start on, which is why the pivot sits behind the foreground rather than nearer. Relative to the photograph everything grows, the foreground most. At 0.2, in the desktop preview, the background measured 2% smaller in the finished window and the children's band 2.5% larger.

The guide frame is fixed on the screen, like a viewfinder's: the photograph's outline as seen from the spot looking straight ahead, its long side along the screen's. It is placed from the camera's pose every frame, so it never lags or drifts, and turns a quarter under rotation lock. Holding the phone sideways and level (within `matchTolerance` of pitch), with the photograph's corners within `matchTolerance` (4°) of the frame's on screen, for `matchHold` (0.6 s) starts the activation. The error is what the viewer sees, so stepping and aiming both move the photograph in the frame; a viewer 10 cm aside who turns back to it still matches, with about 1° of keystone. During the hold the memory eases onto the phone, standing where it stands and turned to where it points, so the photograph settles exactly into the frame and the cut lands there; the street turns with it and its ground floats by at most the remaining offset. The memory stays level, so a tilted phone cannot match. A hint names the largest correction (turn sideways, face the photograph, hold the phone level, walk up, step back, turn left or right, raise or lower the phone, hold still) and turns to read upright at the physical bottom of the screen, also when rotation lock keeps the page in portrait. The match waits for NORMAL tracking.

## People

While the viewer lines the photograph up, phone sideways, a loose pile of eight photographs of people who lived in the old Chinatown sits in the bottom-right corner of the screen as held (`src/memory-cards.js`, styled in `src/memory-cards.css`, people in `src/memory-people.js`). The originals are in `input/people/`; the pile and the album's grid use 400 px tall copies in `input/people/cards/`, and close-ups use copies 1200 px on their long side in `input/people/prints/`. Peter SooHoo's print is on top. The pile stays upright, clear of the guide frame and of the safe areas, also under rotation lock. The compact hint sits at the top of the screen, because the frame leaves only thin margins.

**The album.** Tapping the pile opens it: the prints are dealt from the top of the pile into a grid (4 × 2 sideways, 2 × 4 upright) under the title "People who lived here". Tapping a print carries it, growing, to a large place on the left, with its title and sample text beside it (lorem ipsum, each person's `about`). The photograph or **All photos** puts it back; **Done** or Escape sends every print home to the pile. The match waits while the album is open. Taps on the pile and the album never place or reset the memory, and they suppress WebXR select events.

**The arrival.** When the match holds, the rest of the pile steps aside and Peter's print waits alone for 0.36 s while its shadow lifts. A 3D print (`src/memory-flight.js`) then takes its place exactly, in place, size, tilt and border; the page would draw over it, so nothing may be left there. It glides through the street's space into the photograph for 2.4 s, in one unbroken curve:

- It rises from the pile and draws toward the viewer as it sweeps over to him, to about 1.4 times its landing size, then comes down into the photograph along the line of sight to his eyes from just above it, slowing all the way.
- It tilts on the way, never turning its back on the viewer: its top edge leans back most (up to about 28°) and latest, so it comes in tilted and lies flat only as it touches; its leading edge recedes as it crosses, and it banks slightly as it rises. The paper flexes a little mid-flight.
- Its soft shadow falls on the photograph from a light in front, faint and wide while it is far off, gathering and darkening as it comes in. Only the photograph receives it.
- It keeps one physical size the whole way; it grows and shrinks only by perspective.

It lands with the point between its eyes over his in the restored street (`ARRIVAL`, measured in both photographs), at his face's own size, 1.5 mm in front of the photograph. The touch starts the activation, timed from the touch. As the colour spreads from the photograph's centre, where he stands, its border fades and then the print, gone by 28% of the colour, so his face stays put as the print becomes the man in the street.

The motion follows the stageshow kit (`~/.claude/skills/stageshow`). The pile is dealt on its arrival curve, `cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.8, 0.25, 1)` over 0.8 s, 60 ms apart, and leaves on its travel curve, `cubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.1, 1)`. The album's prints and the 3D flight use its `Fly` curves for things that grow as they travel. `prefers-reduced-motion` drops the pile's and the album's movement. Replay and Reset gather the print back into the pile unseen. The tuning dock's Replay starts the activation without the flight.

**The plate holder.** The historical photograph stands in an old wooden plate holder, cut from a Tripo model of one by `scripts/extract_plate.py` into `input/plate/photo-plate.glb` (273 KB):

- the holder only, with its dark slide removed and the hole cut through;
- the opening stretched across its span only, to the photograph's exact 4:3;
- the photograph at the slide's depth, behind the frame's lip.

`src/memory-plate.js` fits the opening to the photograph and lights the holder in its own shader, so the scene needs no lights. It draws before the occluding matte, which would otherwise clip its back. From 80% of the colour to the end of the flat hold it dissolves outward from the photograph, broken by noise, with a thin cream edge, and is gone before the street opens. To rebuild it, run `.venv/bin/python -I scripts/extract_plate.py <tripo model>.glb input/plate/photo-plate.glb`.

## Memory activation

`input/apalabassa-original.jpg` is the supplied black-and-white photograph of the south side of Apalabassa St. (300 to 320), copied without modification. Vite bundles it as a hashed asset; `photoUrl` selects it.

`input/apalabassa-soohoo.webp` is the restored color photograph used for the SOG reconstruction (`output/apalabassa-soohoo/`, assets in `src/scene-assets.js`), with Peter SooHoo standing in the street; it replaced `input/apalabassa.jpg`, the restoration with children and a dog, on the `claude/soohoo-cards` branch. `colorPhotoUrl` selects it for the activation surface and particle spray. Each particle carries its originating pixel's color while its motion varies. The surface's chromatic refraction samples neighboring RGB channels from this image, and the splats retain their own reconstruction colors. There is no generated pink/cyan palette. Update this color asset when changing the reconstruction.

The stages are controlled by `src/memory-settings.js`:

1. **Dormant:** the original, 0.49 × 0.37 m, in its plate holder, **0.8 m beyond the matching spot** at the matching height, fitting the sideways screen at 80% of its height from the spot. It shows the region the color restoration was cut from (`originalFrame`), so features keep their places as the color arrives. The guide frame and a hint at the bottom of the screen lead the viewer to the spot (see **Match cut**), and the splat scene is hidden.
2. **Color (4.2 s):** a wide, irregular circular wave travels from the center in physical coordinates, independent of photo aspect. The photograph refracts and dissolves through its own restored colors; an 11,516-particle spray curls around the crest. The actual colored splats are revealed on a thin sheet behind the window. Ambient sweep/periphery effects begin with the first colored region at the configurable flat-stage strength.
3. **Surface hold (0.8 s):** the colored reconstruction stays flat before opening while the plate holder dissolves (see **People**).
4. **Depth (3.1 s):** a quintic minimum-jerk curve starts slowly, accelerates, and slows to a stop. The aperture recedes in inverse depth from the photograph to the portal, **3.85 m wide × 2.89 m tall, 3.15 m past the spot**, standing on the floor, and from the spot it grows evenly from the photograph's frame to the portal's (×1.99). The flat splats match the photograph pixel for pixel; as the window opens, each moves from its pixel to its place in the street, the foreground faster than the frame and the background slower: the dolly zoom. With `dolly=0` and the photographer's own frame the street replaces the photograph without moving at all. Moving reveals the depth.

**Street placement.** The settled street is the reconstruction itself at its captured, approximately metric scale times **Scene size** (`sceneScale`, default 1.04), with its capture camera just behind the matching spot by the dolly's offset. `scripts/fit_ground.py` fits SHARP's street ground: the photographer's level camera was 1.39 m above it, and the reconstructed street begins 2.97 m ahead. With Peter in the street SHARP sees the scene about 0.8 of the size it saw the restoration without him (1.70 m and 3.78 m), and he comes out about 1.84 m tall, so this scale is likely the better one. Scene size 1.04 puts the photographer's eye 1.445 m above the street, the matching height first tuned on a phone at scene size 0.85 with the earlier reconstruction; the spot now stands at **Match height** (`matchHeight`, default 1.55 m) and the street 0.104 m lower than it, so its ground stays on the floor. The street's start is 3.09 m from the photographer, 0.81 m in front of the portal. The street is levelled onto the estimated floor along the placement heading; the 0.6° by which the capture camera was not quite level is carried by the whole stage, so the photograph stays square to it and the portal's sill sits within about 3 cm of the floor.

**Ground spill.** The street in front of the portal spills out like daylight through a doorway, keeping its splats as they are. The lit area is a fan whose edges leave the portal's sides at half the **Spill angle** (`spillAngle`, 20–170°, default 31°). Like a penumbra, the edges are a smooth sigmoid with soft tails that widens the further it is from the portal, by the **Spill edge blur** angle (`spillSoftness`, 0–80°, default 60°). Brightness falls off with distance as the form factor from the floor to the opening does, through an exposure curve set by **Ground spill** (`spillStrength`, 0–8×, default 6.3×): it is full at the threshold and rolls off smoothly instead of clipping into a flat plateau; 0 cuts the street at the portal plane. About 1.5 nearly opaque SHARP splats overlap at each point of the ground (1.2–1.7 across the three assets), so the modifier shares the light's optical depth among them equally rather than fading each splat by the light, which would compound into harder edges; per-splat shares made neighbouring splats fade unevenly. The spill is evenly 15% brighter than the street, the street's cut-off front edge fades in with a C2 curve over most of the spilled strip, and the spill never reaches behind the placement point. Everything in front of the portal spills, ground or not. With the street near the photographer's distance, only its first 0.81 m lies in front of the portal, so the spill is modest. Splats that settle hidden fade out early in the expansion instead of crossing the room. While the aperture recedes, ground bound for the floor just in front of the portal is still behind it, so the matte would hide it below the opening and leave a gap. Such splats are held just in front of the aperture during the expansion, slid along the viewer's line of sight so their place and size on screen are unchanged.

The matte occludes each splat at its centre's depth only, so a large splat just behind the portal could paint past the opening onto the floor in front of it. The modifier drops splats behind the aperture whose centres the viewer cannot see through it, every frame; the matte trims the rest per pixel.

The GPU modifier changes uniforms during activation instead of uploading new splat buffers each frame. It transforms covariance with the spatial mapping's Jacobian, preserving each Gaussian's projected footprint instead of separately mixing thin scales and orientations. This fixes gaps during expansion; a small temporary overlap reserve also protects the decimated samples. Spark's covariance renderer and extended precision retain the resulting shapes and positions. This uses more GPU memory than the checkpoint's packed renderer, so mobile performance needs verification. The photo, splats, and particle spray share the same wave. Aperture growth scales the existing matte instead of rebuilding geometry each frame.

The desktop preview includes a floor grid and reviews through a phone held sideways at the matching height, walked forward from the placement with **Walk forward**. The phone retains 8th Wall's calibrated projection. Because the settled street is placed rigidly, ambient effects use the live camera directly once depth opens.

Depth uses `1 / mix(1 / sheetDepth, 1 / targetDepth, growth)` during expansion. Interpolating metres had made distant background splats reach most of their visible parallax change while the aperture had barely grown. The new mapping interpolates the perspective quantity that controls apparent depth; its analytic derivative also drives the covariance transformation, keeping splat footprints coordinated with their centers. The flat shell and fully expanded scene retain their exact endpoint mappings. Its inverse-depth progression equals the aperture's eased progression. `src/memory-placement.js` moves the aperture in the same inverse depth, from the photograph to the portal.

Each flat splat sits on the viewing ray through its pixel **on** the photograph. An earlier version took the ray through a point 6 mm behind it at the same offset, which is a narrower ray: the flat image came out 1.5% smaller than the photograph and the street, and swelled back during the expansion.

Flattening every splat onto exactly the same depth destroys its foreground/background blend order. Spark sorts by distance to the viewer; a coplanar image therefore becomes smeared even with `fx=none`, then abruptly sharpens as the first depth movement restores ordering. The flat stage uses a shallow camera-centered shell: each splat stays on the viewing ray through its anchored photograph pixel, while its hidden distance increases with its original capture distance. The covariance follows the same mapping, so its projected footprint is preserved. This retains the capture's blend order while the image looks flat, then smoothly interpolates to the 3D mapping at the final portal position. The flat-stage gain separately controls the actual sweep/periphery modulation.

`captureAspect` and `captureFocalRatio` describe **the color photograph that produced the SOG**, not the original. `originalFrame` is where that photograph's frame lies in the original: the restoration cropped about 6% off the original's top and 4% off its bottom and stretched it to 4:3. `.venv/bin/python scripts/register_original.py input/apalabassa-original.jpg input/apalabassa-soohoo.webp` measures it from the facades' edges. Current values match `input/apalabassa-soohoo.webp` at 1448×1086 and SHARP's assumed 30mm focal length (1255 px, recorded in the PLY's intrinsics). `captureGroundUp`, `captureCameraHeight`, and `captureGroundStart` describe its street, from `.venv/bin/python scripts/fit_ground.py output/apalabassa-soohoo/apalabassa-soohoo.ply`. Update them all if the reconstruction changes. To rebuild the assets: convert the WebP to PNG (`output/sharp-input/apalabassa-soohoo.png`), then `.venv/bin/sharp predict -i output/sharp-input/apalabassa-soohoo.png -o output/apalabassa-soohoo --device mps`, then PlayCanvas `splat-transform` with `-d 50%` and `-d 25%` into `output/apalabassa-soohoo/decimated/`, and each PLY to `public/apalabassa-soohoo{,_50,_25}.sog`.

## Build and delivery

`npm run build` produces `dist/`, including the AR page, both previews, original photograph, and unmodified 8th Wall engine. SOG files under `public/` are currently ignored by Git. This feature uses the existing SOG assets; it does not regenerate them.

The production project is `chinatown-splat-ar`, served at `https://chinatown-splat-ar.vercel.app`. Its root opens AR with the chosen defaults and a dormant photograph; the tuning URL's `t=0.86` inspection frame is not a playback default. `/ar.html` is also available directly.

For a complete release, link the existing project with `vercel link --yes --project chinatown-splat-ar`, run `npm run vercel:prepare`, then `vercel deploy --prebuilt --prod`. The preparation script verifies all three local SOGs before building, copies only `dist/` into Vercel's static Build Output API structure, and routes `/` to `/ar.html`. This includes Git-ignored scene/engine assets without uploading source folders, local credentials, or development captures. A source-only/Git rebuild must supply the SOG assets separately. The packaged engine keeps its resources and license intact.

Desktop browser verification covers the shader compilation, all activation stages, tap hit testing, sideways viewing, reset/replay, and a 390×844 viewport. Actual mobile camera tracking, Safari rendering, and frame rate require a device check. Existing GitHub and Vercel terminal sessions are used for sync and publication; only an expired or missing login needs user interaction.

To check the match cut, load `/memory.html?controls=1&fx=none&walk=1&t=5` and scrub to 8.1 s: the window grows ×1.99, the children more and the buildings less. In a 4:3 preview window, `&dolly=0&matchFrame=0.923` is the photographer's own frame, and nothing should move: on desktop the flat and finished frames differ by 0.04/255 on average.

To check the flat/depth boundary, load `/memory.html?controls=1&t=5&fxTime=3&flatEffectStrength=0.25` and scrub between 4.9 and 5.1 s. Also compare 5.0 and 5.01 s, immediately around expansion's start. Repeat with `&fx=none` to isolate splat ordering from ambient effects and use the sideways slider to check that the flat image stays anchored when the viewing direction changes.

## WebXR tracking and App Clips

`/ar.html` chooses its tracker when it loads. If the browser offers WebXR `immersive-ar`, the page runs a WebXR session and the host's native tracking drives the camera: ARCore in Chrome on Android, or ARKit inside an iOS App Clip that adds WebXR, such as Needle Go. Otherwise it loads 8th Wall as before; Safari on iPhone has no WebXR AR. `?tracking=webxr` or `?tracking=xr8` forces one, and `?debug=1` shows which one is running on the `tracker` line.

On iPhone, open the page through Needle Go's App Clip: `https://appclip.needle.tools/ar?url=` followed by the URL-encoded page address. Needle's App Clip is already published, so it needs no Apple setup, and page changes deploy to Vercel as usual without an App Store review.

Under WebXR the host draws the camera behind a transparent canvas, and `#ar-ui` is the session's DOM overlay, so the prompts, buttons and tuning dock stay usable. The floor is `local-floor`'s y = 0 when the host grants it. Otherwise it is the first surface hit straight below the session's starting point, or 1.45 m below that point until a surface is found. Memory preparation finishes before Start is enabled: while a session presents, three.js renders every frame with the XR camera, including the warm-up renders. 8th Wall's engine is not downloaded on this path, and its credit line is hidden.

On the dev server, `/ar.html?xremu=1` installs the IWER WebXR emulator (a dev dependency) so the WebXR path runs in a desktop browser. The emulator raises its own view over the page while presenting, so script taps on `#ar-tap` to place the memory. Vite drops this code from production builds.
